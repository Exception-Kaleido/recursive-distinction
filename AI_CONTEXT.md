# AI Context: Recursive Distinction v0.1

## One-sentence summary

This is a frozen toy experiment studying Recursive Distinction: how distinction-trace-reentry dynamics can stabilize, revise, split, and obsolete label-free objecthood candidates.

## Core mechanism

Distinction leaves trace.  
Trace re-enters distinction.  
Repeated re-entry stabilizes provisional objecthood candidates.

## What this experiment claims

- Label-free sensorimotor traces can form objecthood candidates.
- Conflict between existing candidates and new action-outcome traces can trigger candidate splitting.
- Candidate splitting improves adaptation in an ambiguous role-reversal world.
- Objecthood is treated as a revisable commitment, not as a fixed label.

## What this experiment does not claim

- It does not claim consciousness.
- It does not claim selfhood.
- It does not claim general intelligence.
- It does not claim biological plausibility.
- It does not claim that Recursive Distinction is superior to all RL, clustering, or latent-cause baselines.

## Core terms

- Distinction: a selected difference that becomes relevant for action.
- Trace: the remaining record of a distinction-action-outcome event.
- Re-entry: the process by which past traces alter future distinctions.
- Objecthood candidate: a provisional, revisable candidate for “something object-like” in a label-free world.
- Conflict: mismatch between an existing objecthood candidate and new action-outcome traces.
- Splitting: branching an existing candidate when conflicting traces can no longer be absorbed by the same candidate.
